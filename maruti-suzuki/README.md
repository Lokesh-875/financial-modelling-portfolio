# Maruti Suzuki India Ltd — Financial Modelling & Valuation

**Sector: Automobiles (Passenger Vehicles)**

## Overview
Maruti Suzuki India Ltd is India's largest passenger vehicle manufacturer. This project builds a 10-year historical financial statement analysis (FY17–FY26) plus a full 3-statement forecast and DCF valuation — the longest historical window of the three projects, capturing a full economic cycle including the COVID-19 dip.

## Key Historical Metrics (FY17–FY26)

| Metric | FY17 | FY19 | FY21 (COVID) | FY24 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales (₹ Cr) | 68,085 | 86,069 | 70,372 | 141,858 | 183,316 |
| EBITDA Margin | 15.3% | 12.8% | 7.7% | 13.1% | 11.7% |
| Net Margin | 11.0% | 8.9% | 6.2% | 9.5% | 8.0% |

**Observation:** Sales dipped from ₹86,069 Cr (FY19) to ₹70,372 Cr (FY21) during COVID, with EBITDA margin compressing to 7.7% — then recovered sharply, with margins peaking at 13.2% in FY25 before easing slightly in FY26. This cyclicality is a key talking point on why autos carry a higher cost of capital.

| Output | Value |
|---|---|

| PV of Terminal Value | ₹33,674 Cr |
| Value of Operating Assets | ₹52,050 Cr |
| **Equity Value per Share** | **₹1,670.33** |
| Current Market Price | ₹13,250.00 |

**Finding:** The widest DCF-to-market gap of the three projects (~6.9x) — consistent with a business trading at a growth/franchise premium that a plain DCF with a higher, sector-appropriate WACC understates. Useful contrast point against Asian Paints and Titan when discussing how sector risk profile changes valuation inputs.
